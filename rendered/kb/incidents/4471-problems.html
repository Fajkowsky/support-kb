parser: the shared parser reads every date month first

Every tenant on file writes the day first. The parser reads 02/12 as 12 February when the tenant meant 2 December. In every report that quotes two dates, both day numbers are 12 or lower, so the misread parses cleanly and nothing raises an error. Fixed in fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. If a day-first tenant still reports a flip, check that the fix asks the locale, as causes.ts does, rather than a list of EU locales: en-GB writes the day first too.

hint: booking-v3’s form tells people to type the month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. A day-first tenant’s people type what the hint says, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way. The symptom matches the parser bug, but the parser fix does not touch this code path. Only booking-v3 tenants, and only reports that came in through the form. Won’t fix: the booking-v3 form is retired.

one-day move: the date shifts by a day, day and month stay put

These reports describe a different failure. Tuesday shows as Monday. A client in Lisbon gets Tuesday while a client in Toronto gets Monday for the same meeting. An invite arrives before its booking. June bookings are one day off. The desk’s notes already flag them, but the cause table on #4471 files them under parser because it looks up the tenant only. A time-zone or daylight-saving conversion at a date boundary fits these reports. That is a lead, not a finding. Still open: the parser fix reads day and month in the right order, and a shifted day has both right.

How the reports were counted

  • One report per id. The ticket system exports overlapping pages, so some reports appear twice.
  • Cause by tenant: locale and booking form from the tenant directory, through causes.ts. Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser. It disagrees with the tenant often, so it decides nothing here.
  • One-day moves from desk-notes.json, counted on their own and taken out of parser.

Lessons

  • Merge on cause, not on symptom. All three problems produce the same symptom, a wrong date, so every subject line looked alike. A new decision record should replace 0001; 0001 stays as written.
  • Read what the page shows. The cause table on #4471 already showed a second cause while the words above it said one issue.
